14  How priority is actually computed here

Your position in line is a weighted sum of factors. On MedicineBow the weights are QOS × 200, fair-share × 100, job-size × 100, age × 10. QOS dominates — it’s weighted twice fair-share and twenty times age. Translation: which QOS you pick matters more than anything else you do. You cannot out-wait a better QOS by sitting in the queue.

14.1 The real formula (straight from the machine)

Slurm computes a priority score for every pending job and runs the highest scores first (subject to backfill, below). The score is a weighted sum, and MedicineBow’s actual weights are:

PriorityWeightQOS       = 200     <- dominant
PriorityWeightFairShare = 100
PriorityWeightJobSize   = 100
PriorityWeightAge       = 10
PriorityWeightPartition = 0       (partition doesn't affect priority here)

Each factor is a number between 0 and 1, multiplied by its weight, and summed. So the biggest lever, by a wide margin, is QOS.

14.2 What each factor means for you

QOS (weight 200) — the big one. Higher-priority QOS = dramatically better position. From Chapter 12: interactive (90) > fast (80) > normal (70) > long (60). Choosing fast over long is worth more priority than any amount of waiting will earn you. This is why the short-vs-long decision in Chapter 13 matters so much.

Fair-share (weight 100) — recent usage. Slurm tracks how much your account has used lately, relative to its share. Burn a lot this week and your fair-share factor drops, lowering priority for a while; it recovers as usage decays (half-life ~7 days here). You can’t game this — it’s the mechanism that stops any one group from monopolizing the machine. It rewards steady use over bursty hogging.

Job size (weight 100). A factor based on the resources requested. Combined with the SMALL_RELATIVE_TO_TIME flag this cluster sets, it interacts with your time request — another reason not to over-ask (Chapter 11).

Age (weight 10) — time spent waiting. Your job slowly gains priority the longer it sits, maxing out after ~3 days. But at weight 10, age is a tie-breaker, not a strategy. You will not age your way past someone who chose a better QOS. Waiting is the weakest lever there is.

14.3 The one practical takeaway

Pick the QOS, don’t play the queue

Because QOS is weighted 2× fair-share and 20× age, the highest-leverage thing you can do for start time is choose the most appropriate high-priority QOS your job fits into — the shortest walltime tier that still lets your job finish (Chapter 13). Everything else — waiting longer, resubmitting, submitting at 3am — is noise by comparison.

14.4 Backfill: why a small job can jump the line

Priority decides the order, but Slurm also backfills: if the top job is waiting for, say, 40 nodes to free up, Slurm will slot in a smaller, shorter job that fits in the gap right now — as long as doing so won’t delay that top job’s reserved start time. This is why a tiny 20-minute test can start immediately even when the queue looks deep, and it’s a big part of why short jobs feel so much snappier than long ones. Requesting less time and fewer resources makes your job backfill-eligible in more gaps — yet another payoff for right-sizing.

14.5 Seeing your own priority

sprio -j <jobid>            # the priority breakdown for a pending job
sprio -l                    # all pending jobs, with factor columns
showjobreasons <jobid>      # ARCC helper: plain-language "why am I waiting?"

If sprio shows your QOS factor is low because you’re on long, and the job would actually finish in 10 hours, resubmitting under fast is the single most effective change you can make.
