# ARCC / MedicineBow — Findings

Research notes for the "how to use ARCC" book aimed at NSF **ASCEND/ARID** performers.

Two independent sources, and **they disagree in places**:

- **[LIVE]** — harvested by running commands on `mblog2.arcc.uwyo.edu`, 2026-07-14. This is
  ground truth about the machine *as it is today*.
- **[DOCS]** — the official ARCC wiki. **The docs moved**: the current site is
  <https://pages.arcc.uwyo.edu/arcc-wiki/main/>. The old Atlassian Confluence wiki is
  **stale** (it still describes retired clusters) — treat it as history.

Where they conflict, **[LIVE] wins** and I've flagged it. Those conflicts are some of the
most useful content in the book, because a novice following the docs will hit them.

## 1. The program (this shapes every example in the book)

Corrects my earlier guess — **ASCEND and ARID are not EPSCoR.**

- **ASCEND** = **A**dvanced **S**ensing and **C**omputation for **EN**vironmental
  **D**ecision-making. It's an **NSF Regional Innovation Engine** (the NSF Engines program),
  led by **Innosphere** (Fort Collins accelerator), up to **$45M**, spanning the I-25 corridor
  from Laramie to Colorado Springs. Originally branded the *Colorado–Wyoming Climate
  Resilience Engine* — same award, if you see both names. Partners include **UW, CSU, CU
  Boulder, Mines, NCAR, and NEON**.
- **ARID** = **A**sset **R**esilience through **I**ntelligent **D**igital Twins — one of
  ASCEND's flagship R&D programs (siblings: SHIELD for soil health, and an Extreme Weather
  Prediction program launching 2027). ARID protects **power and water utilities from wildfire
  risk**: high-res wildfire risk characterization, physics-informed ignition prediction,
  near-real-time fire weather modeling, quantum-enhanced thermal imaging for early detection,
  and post-wildfire water quality monitoring — fused into a **digital twin**.

So the science is **wildfire + atmospheric + geospatial modeling**, and the book's worked
examples should be exactly that: WRF-style fire-weather runs (MPI, many-core), ML surrogates
and PINNs (GPU), big ensemble sweeps (job arrays), and raster/remote-sensing pipelines
(GDAL/GEOS, which are both installed).

**Still unverified:** UW's specific deliverable inside ARID. And `cowy-firesurro` — the
project this repo lives in — has no public record; it reads as *CO-WY fire surrogate*, which
fits ARID perfectly, but **that's inference. Confirm with the PI.**

### Accounts — the part that surprises people

- **You cannot request your own account.** A **PI** must request it, and ARCC will not act
  without written PI confirmation. No project → no account, full stop. For ASCEND/ARID
  performers this is *the* first blocker, so it belongs on page one of the book.
- PI must be UW faculty (extended-term) or a Research Scientist. Not adjunct, not emeritus.
- Non-UW collaborators get **ARCC-Only accounts** — HPC + GitLab only, and critically
  **they cannot use Globus** (they need a guest collection instead). Given ASCEND is a
  multi-institution consortium, this will bite CSU/CU/NCAR collaborators constantly.
- Free tier: **100,000 CPU core-hours/yr** and **20,000 GPU-hours/yr** per researcher.

### SSH is key-and-certificate, not password

**You must log into the web portal first** — SSH keys are generated by the OnDemand **SSH Key
Manager**, downloaded as a zip, and unzipped into `~/.ssh`:

```bash
unzip ~/Downloads/<username>.zip -d ~/.ssh
chmod 0600 -R ~/.ssh
ssh-add
```

**Keys expire after one year** and must be re-downloaded — a great "why did my login suddenly
break?" FAQ entry. 2FA is Duo (portal only; settings at `wyosecure.uwyo.edu`). **No VPN is
required**, on or off campus.

## 3. Storage — three filesystems, and nothing is backed up

**[LIVE]** from `arccquota`, cross-checked against **[DOCS]**:

| Path | Quota | Snapshots | **Backups** | Purge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/home/$USER` | **50 GB** | yes | **NO** | only when you leave UW |
| `/project/<proj>` | **5 TB** | yes | **NO** | 6 months after project ends |
| `/gscratch/$USER` (`$SCRATCH`) | **5 TB** | yes | **NO** | **90 days of inactivity** |
| `/project/<proj>/software` | **+250 GB** (on top) | | | — |
| `/lscratch` | node-local, compute nodes | — | — | **wiped at end of each job** |

**⚠️ The single most important sentence in the storage chapter: NOTHING IS BACKED UP.** Not
home, not project, not scratch. There are *snapshots*, but those live on the same VAST array —
if the array dies, they die. ARCC is explicit about this distinction and most users miss it.
(Snapshot restore within 20 days: email `arcc-help@uwyo.edu`.) The separate **Alcova** service
*does* have real nightly backups.

Two conflicts worth knowing:

- **⚠️ `/gscratch` purge: 90 days, not 45.** The current wiki FAQ says 90 days of inactivity;
  the legacy Confluence storage policy says 45. Both are live on the web. **90 is current.**
- **⚠️ `/data` doesn't exist.** Docs describe it as login-node-only storage; **[LIVE]** on
  mblog2 there is no `/data`. `/lscratch` *does* exist.

**[LIVE] confirmed** `/project/cowy-firesurro/software` exists — that's the extra 250 GB, and
ARCC wants you to install software *there*, **not** in `/home`. Given the 50 GB home quota, a
single conda env can eat your entire home directory. This is the #1 self-inflicted wound and
deserves a loud callout box.

## 4. Slurm

### The one directive you cannot omit

```bash
#SBATCH --account=cowy-firesurro    # your PROJECT name, not your username
```

**[DOCS]**: every job must specify an account. This is the *only* strictly-required directive
on MedicineBow, and it's the first error every new user hits. `salloc` additionally requires
`-A` and `-t`.

### How priority is actually computed — **[LIVE]**, and undocumented anywhere

`scontrol show config` gives the real formula, which **[DOCS] never publishes** (the research
agent explicitly could not find it):

```
PriorityType            = priority/multifactor
PriorityWeightQOS       = 200      <- dominant
PriorityWeightFairShare = 100
PriorityWeightJobSize   = 100
PriorityWeightAge       = 10
MaxArraySize            = 10000
```

**QOS is weighted 2× fairshare and 20× age.** That single fact is the spine of the scheduling
chapter: *which QOS you choose matters more than anything else you do.*

### QOS **[LIVE]** (`sqos`)

| QOS | Priority | Max walltime | CPU cap / account |
|---|---|---|---|
| `inv-*` (investor) | **110** | 14 d | varies |
| `interactive` | 90 | 8 h | 4746 |
| `fast` | 80 | 12 h | 4746 |
| `normal` *(default)* | 70 | 3 d | 4746 |
| `long` | 60 | 7 d | 2876 |
| `extended` | 50 | 14 d | 2157 |
| `debug` | 0 | 1 h | cpu=7, mem=32G, node=2 |

There's a clean, teachable tradeoff: **the longer the walltime you ask for, the lower your
priority *and* the smaller your allowed footprint.** 14 days → priority 50 and a third of the
CPU cap. 12 hours → priority 80 and the full cap. Every QOS is `DenyOnLimit`, so an
over-limit job is **rejected at submit time**, not silently queued forever. That's good news
and worth saying out loud.

This account (`cowy-firesurro`) may use: `debug, fast, interactive, long, normal`
(`sacctmgr show assoc user=$USER format=account,qos`).

### Partitions **[LIVE]** (`sinfo -e`, `showpartitions`)

| Partition | Nodes | Cores/node | Mem/node | GPUs/node |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`mb`** *(default)* | 25 | 96 (EPYC 9454) | 1023 GB | — |
| `mb-h100` | 6 | 96 | 1281 GB | 8× **H100** |
| `mb-l40s` | 4 | 96 | 765 GB | 8× L40S (one node: 4) |
| `mb-a30` | 8 | 96 | 765 GB | 8× A30 |
| `mb-a6000` | 1 | 64 | 1023 GB | 4× A6000 |
| `mb-r6000` | 3 | 72 | 515 GB | 4× R6000 |
| `debug` | 4 | 28 | 128 GB | — |

Beartooth, Teton, and WildIris are **retired as clusters** and now exist only as MedicineBow
partitions (`teton` alone is 250 nodes). Also `inv-*` = investor-owned hardware. A separate
**AI4WY** cluster (NVIDIA GraceHopper) exists for AI work.

### ⚠️ The memory trap — **[LIVE]**, and I'd bet money this burns people

Partition `mb` sets **`DefMemPerCPU=10000`** (10 GB), against a global default of 3584 MB. So:

```bash
#SBATCH --cpus-per-task=8      # and no --mem
```
silently requests **80 GB**. You then queue for a number you never typed. Always set `--mem`
explicitly.

### ⚠️ `--constraint=non-investor` DOES NOT WORK — but a `non-investor` *partition* does **[LIVE, 2026-07-27]**

**[DOCS]** tells you to use `--constraint=non-investor` to avoid preemption. **[LIVE]** I
checked every node's feature string on the cluster and **no node advertises a `non-investor`
feature.** That constraint would match nothing. The docs are stale here.

**RESOLVED — the real mechanism is a partition, not a constraint.** Re-checking on
2026-07-27, `sinfo`/`showpartitions` show two partitions the earlier pass missed:

- **`non-investor`** — 110 CPU nodes (`mbcpu-013-024`, plus curated teton/beartooth nodes),
  `AllowQos=ALL`, MaxTime 7 d.
- **`non-investor-gpu`** — 7 GPU nodes, `QoS=require-gpu`.

So the correct advice is **`--partition=non-investor`** (or `non-investor-gpu`) for work you
don't want preempted. This resolves open question #1. The `teton`, `beartooth`, `wildiris`,
and `teton-gpu`/`beartooth-gpu` partitions also exist as first-class partitions (not just
"absorbed clusters").

The underlying concern is real and belongs in the book: MedicineBow is a **condo model**. If
you land on investor hardware, **the investor can reclaim it at any moment and your job is
automatically requeued.** Idle investor nodes are effectively a backfill queue. Anyone
running long jobs needs to know this, and needs to checkpoint.

### ARCC's own commands **[LIVE]** — on `$PATH` by default, and undiscoverable

`/apps/s/arcc/1.0/bin`: `arccquota` · `arccjobs` · `showjob` · `shownode` · `showpartitions` ·
`showuserjobs` · `showuserlimits` · `showjobreasons` · `sqos` · `gpuavail` · `slurm_gpustat` ·
`nvtop` · `pestat` · `psjob` · `psnode` · `joblist` · `jobqos`

These deserve a chapter. `showjobreasons` and `showuserlimits` answer the two questions every
frustrated newcomer has — *why isn't my job running?* and *what am I even allowed to ask
for?* — and each is a single word.

**⚠️ `arccq` does not exist** despite **[DOCS]** listing it as a `squeue` alternative. **[LIVE]**
confirms it's not installed.

## 5. Software

Lmod 8.7, **hierarchical** — the #1 source of "that module doesn't exist" confusion:

```bash
module avail          # LIES BY OMISSION: only what's reachable right now
module spider gdal    # the truth: what exists, and what to load first
```

- **Core:** `gcc/{11.4.0, 13.2.0, 14.2.0, 15.2.0}`, `nvhpc-sdk`, `oneapi`, `openmpi/5.0.10`
- **Only after loading a compiler:** `python/{3.10.6, 3.12.0, 3.14.4}`, `r/{4.4.0, 4.5.3}`,
  `gdal/{3.7.3, 3.13.0}`, `netcdf-c/4.9.2-ompi`, `hdf5/1.14.3*`,
  `cuda-toolkit/{12.4.1, 12.6.1}`, `openmpi/4.1.6`, `rclone/1.63.1`
- **Standalone:** `apptainer/{1.3.5, 1.4.1}`, `singularity/4.1.0`, `miniconda3/24.3.0`,
  `julia/1.10.3`

**⚠️ A genuine contradiction users must be told about:** `gcc/13.2.0` prints a deprecation
warning telling you to move to `gcc/14.2.0` — but **`cuda-toolkit` is only built against
`gcc/13.2.0`**. GPU users have to knowingly load the deprecated compiler. **[LIVE]**

Other notes: no `sudo`, ever. Conda is supported (no mamba anywhere). Apptainer is the
container runtime; ARCC's containers page is a stub that just links out. Spack is never named
in the docs but the module version strings (`hdf5/1.14.3__hl_True__fortran_False-ompi`) are an
unmistakable Spack fingerprint.

## 6. Data transfer

- **Globus** is the recommended path. Search collections for `uwyo-ARCC` → **"Medicine Bow"**.
  Its root is **`cluster`**, so paths look like `/cluster/medbow/project/cowy-firesurro` —
  which is *exactly* this repo's path, confirming the layout.
- **⚠️ Globus requires a full UWyo account.** ARCC-Only accounts can't use it. For a
  multi-institution consortium like ASCEND this is a real friction point.
- CLI: `scp` / `rsync` / `sftp` to `medicinebow.arcc.uwyo.edu`. ARCC's *recommended* CLI tool
  is **`rclone`** (available as a module).
- **No dedicated DTN** appears anywhere in the docs; transfers go through the login nodes, or
  through Globus.

## 8. Queue-time data **[LIVE, 2026-07-27]** — the centerpiece the book needed

Measured from `sacct` over a 3-day window (~1.7M jobs), wait = Start − Eligible. Snapshot
at capture time: **~2000 running, ~800 pending**, nearly all pending for reason `Priority`.

| Partition | Median wait | p90 | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| `teton` | ~0 s | ~13 m | huge pool; array/throughput starts fast (window included a big `fast` array) |
| `mb` (CPU default) | <1 m | ~10 m | plentiful; short CPU jobs start quickly |
| `beartooth` | ~4 m | ~11 m | steady CPU |
| `mb-a30` (GPU) | ~2 m | ~1.5 h | smaller GPUs, usually gettable |
| `mb-h100` (GPU) | s–hours | **~8–9 h** | bimodal: idle now or a long wait |
| `mb-l40s` (GPU) | ~1 h | ~2–6 h | consistently contended |

By QOS (most representative CPU signal is `normal`, n≈26k): `interactive` median ~11 s /
p90 ~30 s · `normal` median ~19 s / p90 ~8 m · `fast` median ~0 s / p90 ~13 m.

**Takeaway for the book:** CPU work starts fast; GPU (esp. L40S/H100) is where you wait.
Short PoC → `debug`/`fast`/`interactive`; long sims → `long` on `non-investor`, and
checkpoint. This is chapter 13 of the book.

## 9. Proposed book outline

Every chapter states *why* before *how*.

**Part I — Getting oriented**
1. What a cluster *is*, and why your laptop isn't one (shared machine; login vs. compute
   nodes; the cardinal sin of computing on a login node)
2. Getting an account — and why **your PI must do it for you**
3. First login, the web portal, and the SSH key manager (keys expire yearly!)

**Part II — The Linux you actually need** *(no more, no less)*
4. Shell survival kit
5. Files, paths, permissions, and `tmux` — so a dropped connection doesn't kill your work

**Part III — Where your data lives**
6. The three filesystems, and the contract each one offers — **including: nothing is backed up**
7. Moving data: Globus, `rclone`, `rsync` (and the ARCC-Only/Globus trap for collaborators)

**Part IV — Software**
8. Why module systems exist, and how Lmod's hierarchy trips you up (`spider`, not `avail`)
9. Conda without destroying your 50 GB home directory
10. Containers with Apptainer: why, and when

**Part V — Slurm** *(the heart of the book)*
11. Why a scheduler exists at all — the tragedy of the commons, made concrete
12. Anatomy of a batch job (starting with `--account`, the one you can't omit)
13. Asking for resources — and why over-asking punishes **you** first (the 10 GB/CPU trap)
14. Partitions and QOS on MedicineBow
15. How priority is *actually* computed here — QOS 200, fairshare 100, age 10
16. Watching a job: `squeue`, `sacct`, `seff`, `showjobreasons`
17. Job arrays — the one feature that changes how you work
18. Interactive and GPU jobs
19. Preemption, requeue, and the condo model — why long jobs must checkpoint
20. When jobs fail: reading OOM kills, timeouts, node failures

**Part VI — Working well**
21. The parallelism ladder: serial → array → multicore → MPI → GPU
22. Checkpointing and surviving the walltime limit
23. Reproducibility

**Part VII — Reference**
24. Cheat sheet · Glossary · Troubleshooting · Getting help
